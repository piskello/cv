# Research
Matteo Moretti has worked in academia since 2006, first as an adjunct lecturer and then as an RTD-A from 2013 to 2019. In that period he co-founded the [Visual Journalism research platform at the Free University of Bozen-Bolzano](https://visualjournalism.unibz.it), as well as the social design platform publishing [Design for Migration](http://designformigration.com), the first archive of design practices emerging in response to migration. This research led to the monograph *Socio-Social-Design*, published by Corraini (2019). His research outputs have received international awards (Data Journalism Award 2015, European Design Award 2016/17) and helped position the Faculty of Design at unibz internationally. His research bridges professional practice and teaching: working with students, he explores approaches, methods, and strategies later validated in research work. This informs and supports the growth of professional processes in which graduates go on to design.

## Research areas and themes
**Informative Experiences**  
This strand explores design approaches aimed at building informative experiences that make complex phenomena more accessible and understandable in public space. Moving beyond a purely digital view of visual journalism, it investigates hybrid informative practices—physical and phygital—where data, narrative, and interaction converge in situated, transmedia experiences.  
Through inter- and transdisciplinary research groups bringing together researchers, designers, journalists, developers, and social scientists, it develops and evaluates devices, installations, platforms, and narrative formats that combine digital media and physical artefacts. The goal is to explore how design can support collective understanding and informed debate on complex social, environmental, and cultural issues. The research has yielded projects in public, educational, cultural, and institutional settings, contributing to open information ecosystems and immersive experiences oriented toward transparency and civic engagement.

**Digital Social Design**  
This strand explores design practices aimed at social transformation at the intersection of the digital and public space, examining how processes of inclusion, collaboration, and collective knowledge-building move between digital environments and physical contexts.  
Through inter- and transdisciplinary approaches integrating design, social sciences, and media studies, it develops platforms, information ecosystems, and hybrid experiences that connect data, narratives, communities, and territories. The goal is to design conditions that foster accessibility, civic activation, and new forms of digital commons. The research has led to projects adopted by cultural organisations, public institutions, and international networks, supporting collaborative networks and active citizenship practices.

**Participatory Data Physicalization**  
This field explores the potential of participatory data practices, going beyond data physicalization as mere materialisation of information. It examines how data, participation, interaction, and co-construction processes can be integrated within situated devices and experiences, where participants are not only recipients of information but actively contribute to producing, interpreting, and discussing data.  
It is a transdisciplinary research area linking information design, interaction design, product design, social design, exhibit design, and facilitation practices, aiming to explore how design can support collective reflection, situated learning, and social activation. The research has produced projects and installations in public, educational, and institutional contexts, adopted by national and international organisations and institutions, including the Joint Research Centre of the European Commission.

## Coordination of research projects and funding
@years 2017-2020
**De-constructing walls - Design practices for new perspectives on migration**  
*Principal Investigator / Funding: unibz / Amount: €9,900*

From 2017 to 2020 the project collected, analysed, and mapped innovative and effective design experiences in the migration field through the designformigration.com archive. The research then fed into the monograph *Socio– Social– Design*, a first theoretical contribution on new forms of design in migration.

@years 2018-2019
**Das Land**  
*Principal Investigator / Funding: Autonomous Province of South Tyrol / Amount: €50,000*  
Commissioned by the Autonomous Province of South Tyrol, the project examined how a transmedia approach can narrate heritage, focusing on the design of the institutional magazine *Das Land*.

@years 2018-2019
**Take them at your place!**  
*Principal Investigator / Funding: unibz / Amount: €19,000*  
With an interdisciplinary team of anthropologists, photographers, video makers, and journalists, the project explored innovative narrative forms to support debate on widespread hospitality.

@years 2017-2018
**Participatory Data Physicalization**  
*Principal Investigator / Funding: Know and Be live / Amount: €13,000*  
In collaboration with partner Know and Be live, the research project explored the informative and reflective potential of data materialisation in cancer prevention.

@years 2015-2015
**Brenner Crossing**  
*Principal Investigator / Funding: unibz / Amount: €7,500*  
The project explored new narrative approaches to the complexity of migration phenomena through interdisciplinary collaboration among designers, anthropologists, and archaeologists. The research culminated in curating *Europa Dreaming*.

@years 2014-2015
**People’s Republic of Bolzano**  
*Principal Investigator / Funding: unibz / Amount: €3,500*  
The project inaugurated the visual journalism research strand at the Free University of Bozen-Bolzano, bringing an interdisciplinary team at the intersection of design and social sciences to produce an interactive counter-narrative supporting more informed debate and mitigating stereotypes about the Chinese community in Bolzano.

# Professional practice
Since 2000 he has worked in information design, digital communication, and data-driven storytelling.  
In 2020 he co-founded Sheldon.studio srl, a studio specialising in data visualisation, informative experiences, and digital social design, working with cultural institutions, public bodies, and international organisations.
